# GigFlow Economic Role

**Category:** Nearly sellable → direct-revenue business

## Economic objective
Make GigFlow economically productive in the gig economy by solving worker and operator problems that create clear willingness to pay, rather than expanding features without a commercial path.

## Target customer
Gig workers, independent contractors, multi-app workers, small operators, and eventually platforms or service businesses that need better workflow, income, opportunity, or operating coordination.

## Problem worth paying for
Fragmented gig work creates income volatility, scheduling friction, opportunity discovery problems, administrative burden, and weak decision support. GigFlow should help users make better economic decisions and execute work with less friction.

## Distribution
Direct web/mobile product, gig-worker communities, referral loops, partnerships with service providers, creator/community distribution, and B2B channels where the same infrastructure serves operators.

## Monetization
Premium worker tools where value is obvious, transaction or marketplace revenue where GigFlow creates the transaction, operator/business subscriptions, lead or service fees where aligned, and enterprise/platform licensing where justified.

## Moat
Cross-platform workflow intelligence, accumulated worker outcome data, decision support, automation, and a system designed around the worker's economic outcome rather than a single marketplace's incentives.

## Immediate economic blockers
Sharpen the first paid wedge, define the highest-value user segment, complete the minimum reliable workflow that produces measurable economic benefit, establish payment/entitlement flow, and put the product in front of real gig workers.

## Next highest-value action
Select the narrowest capability that can demonstrably save or earn users money and launch it. User economic outcome data should determine what gets built next.

## Not now
Do not build broad gig-economy parity features merely because competitors have them. Avoid marketplace complexity until there is evidence that it materially improves distribution or revenue.

## Evidence required
Paid conversion, measurable income/time benefit, retention, repeat usage, referral behavior, and transaction or contract evidence.
